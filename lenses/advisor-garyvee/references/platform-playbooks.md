# Day Trading Attention — Platform Playbooks

## The Modern Advertising Framework (MAF)

Before diving into per-platform strategies, understand the 6-step framework that applies ACROSS all platforms:

1. **Cohort Development** `[DTA:CT]` — Define narrow consumer segmentations ("cohorts with teeth")
2. **Platforms and Culture (PAC)** `[DTA:PN]` — Research platform features + cultural trends your cohorts care about
3. **Strategic Organic Content (SOC)** `[DTA:QQ]` `[DTA:HK]` — Make content informed by PAC, optimized per platform
4. **Amplification** `[DTA:PA]` — Spend against what already works organically
5. **Modern Commercials** — Higher-production videos people actually WANT to watch, informed by SOC insights
6. **Post-Creative Strategy (PCS)** `[DTA:CM]` — Read every comment, gather insights, refine cohorts, repeat

## TikTok

### [DTA:TK] TikTok Strategy
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 4 (TikTok section)
**Core Principle:** TikTok is the platform that changed everything. It popularized the interest-graph algorithm that all platforms now copy. Mastering TikTok content creation will make you better at ALL platforms. The For You page distributes content based on interests, not followers — a 15-follower car dealership can get millions of views. TikTok is the modern-day equivalent of hip-hop culture — it's changing how people communicate, the slang they use, the formats they create in. Don't dismiss it.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: building brand, reaching consumers of any age, learning content creation, testing creative concepts
- AVOID WHEN: never for organic. For paid: if you're B2B with very specialized products, LinkedIn may deliver better immediate ROI
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:TF]`, `[DTA:HK]`, `[DTA:OR]`, `[DTA:QQ]`

### [DTA:TK.1] TikTok First 3 Seconds
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 4 (TikTok section)
**Core Principle:** First 3 seconds are even MORE critical on TikTok than other platforms. This is where the algorithm decides who to distribute your video to, and where users decide if they care. Call out your cohort directly, add curiosity-provoking titles, state strong POVs, use creative camera movements. Make the opening broadly relatable while the rest of the video can go narrow.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Title visible in frame 1 — in-app text for native look
2. Say the title out loud in the first seconds
3. Call out your cohort: "For all the real estate agents out there" or "POV: you're a girl dad"
4. Camera movement or visual change in the first second
5. Feature people who look like your target audience
6. Make opening broadly relatable, then go specific
7. No slow intros, no "hey guys" — get into the message IMMEDIATELY

### [DTA:TK.2] TikTok Search & Community
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 4
**Core Principle:** TikTok search is the ideal place for consumer research. Type your industry terms (e.g., "office supplies," "car dealership") and discover what content formats work, what concerns people have, what language they use. Interest-based communities exist (#booktok, #sportstok, etc.) with their own inside jokes and slang. Search also reveals trending creative formats for your category.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Search industry terms on TikTok before creating content
2. Note trending formats, popular titles, commonly used audio
3. Find category-specific trends (not just broad TikTok trends)
4. Use suggested search terms for content ideas (e.g., "dealership red flags")
5. Follow accounts in your category to train your For You page
6. Leave thoughtful comments on popular posts in your category for awareness
7. Use video replies to comments to build deeper relationships

### [DTA:TK.3] TikTok Native Elements
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Parts 3-4
**Core Principle:** Use TikTok's in-app creative features to make content feel native. CapCut templates (TikTok and CapCut share parent company ByteDance) may get extra distribution. The platform wants users to feel like content was MADE for TikTok. Lighthearted, casual tone — even for serious topics. Use emojis in copy, casual language, in-app fonts, trending audio.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Use TikTok's in-app text features for titles (native font style)
2. Experiment with CapCut templates — may get distribution boost
3. Use trending audio relevant to your category
4. Duet and stitch popular videos for added distribution
5. TikTok carousels: swiping past last slide leads to profile — use "swipe and follow me" CTA
6. Lighthearted tone even for professional topics
7. TikTok Shop for product sales — also explore Shop Retargeting for ads
8. Post multiple times per day — TikTok's algorithm treats each piece of content independently

## Instagram

### [DTA:IG] Instagram Strategy
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 4 (Instagram section)
**Core Principle:** Instagram is the "one-stop shop" — like newspapers, radio, TV, and magazines wrapped into one. It's the "catch-all" for content consumption with 2B+ MAU. Think of your Instagram profile as your modern "home page." The platform is mature and competitive (the "professional league" of social media), so you need to step up your game. Different creative units reach different audiences: Reels reach nonfollowers, carousels/stories/images engage followers. Use ALL creative units.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: building brand, reaching consumers 18-45+, establishing a "home base" for your internet presence
- AVOID WHEN: never — Instagram should be part of every strategy
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:PN]`, `[DTA:HK]`, `[DTA:RP]`, `[DTA:CT]`

### [DTA:IG.1] Instagram Creative Mix
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 4
**Core Principle:** Instagram rewards diversity of creative units. Reels reach nonfollowers. Carousels and images engage existing followers. Stories maintain connection with your audience. Broadcast channels reach people in DMs. Live streams reach guests' audiences. If you only use one format, you're missing whole audience segments. GaryVee's team found that a carousel with a meme as tile 1 and a video as tile 2 reached more accounts than the video alone as a Reel.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Mix Reels (for nonfollower reach) with carousels, images, stories
2. Try meme/quote as carousel tile 1 + video as tile 2 for broader reach
3. "The person who sent you this wants you to know..." format for shares — drives massive reach
4. Use Instagram Highlights to showcase different aspects of your business
5. Set up a broadcast channel for direct-to-DM updates
6. Use collaborative posts with guests/partners to reach their audience
7. Pin up to 3 comments to control the narrative in comments section
8. Share Reels in Reels tab only (not in-feed) as one strategy variation to test
9. Post at different times and test what works
10. Instagram grid thumbnails: First frame of Reel must look good in the grid format (different dimensions)

### [DTA:IG.2] Instagram Profile Hygiene
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Parts 3-4
**Core Principle:** Your Instagram profile is your modern "home page" for the internet. When content goes viral, thousands of people will click through to your profile. If it's not optimized, you're losing potential customers/followers. Bio must explain what you do, links must work, Highlights should showcase key aspects of business, broadcast channel should be accessible.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Full bio describing what you do
2. Website link (or link-in-bio service with multiple links)
3. Phone number if relevant
4. Highlights: Showcase products, testimonials, behind-the-scenes, FAQ
5. Broadcast channel for infrequent but valuable updates
6. Follow related accounts (e.g., if you have stores in multiple cities, follow each other)
7. Pin your best-performing or most representative content at the top of your grid
8. ATL Mixers cautionary tale: 19.9M views on TikTok but link in bio went to Instagram, no website, no phone number, no clear CTA — lost massive revenue

## X (Twitter)

### [DTA:XS] X/Twitter Strategy
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 4 (X/Twitter section)
**Core Principle:** X/Twitter is the "water cooler" of society — where people share opinions and hot takes. It's changed the least of any major platform since 2007. Special strengths: (1) Listening — Twitter Advanced Search is incredibly powerful for finding what people say about your brand, category, or interests. (2) High-volume posting — 10-20+ posts/day is acceptable. (3) Text-first platform good for writers. (4) Threads for long-form. GaryVee built his brand from 2007-2011 by replying to tweets for hours every day.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: building brand through conversation, listening to market, B2B engagement, hot takes, thought leadership
- AVOID WHEN: never — keep as part of strategy even if secondary
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:CM]`, `[DTA:QQ]`, `[DTA:RP]`

### [DTA:XS.1] Twitter for Listening
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 4
**Core Principle:** Twitter Advanced Search is still an incredibly effective tool. Search your brand name, competitor names, industry terms. Find what people are saying and jump into conversations. Search terms related to products/topics to inform content strategy across ALL platforms. This is how GaryVee learned wine consumer preferences (merlot vs. pinot noir from the movie Sideways). Also powerful for surprise and delight — search brand name, find frustrated or happy customers, respond.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Search your brand name daily — respond to mentions
2. Search industry terms to discover consumer sentiment
3. Search competitor names for opportunities
4. Use listening insights to inform content on ALL platforms (not just X)
5. Surprise and delight: Find customers who mention you, respond with something nice
6. Chewy example: Customer posted positive experience → brand engagement → word of mouth
7. Jump into conversations with genuine opinions/expertise

### [DTA:XS.2] Twitter Volume & Formats
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 4
**Core Principle:** Twitter allows and rewards high-volume posting — 10-20+ times/day is fine. Test different formats: short quotes, threads, long posts (X Premium), GIFs, images, video. Strong writers should lean into text. Repurpose LinkedIn text posts, Facebook status updates, YouTube Community posts. Tweet graphics (screenshots of tweets) can be posted as images across other platforms.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Post 10-20+ times/day if capacity allows
2. Mix: short quotes, threads, images, GIFs, video, polls
3. Try different messaging styles to see what gets likes, reposts, replies
4. Screenshot tweets → crop → post as image on Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn
5. Use tweets as YouTube thumbnails or video overlays
6. Long-form writing: Threads or X Premium for extended content
7. Repurpose text posts from other platforms to X

## LinkedIn

### [DTA:LI] LinkedIn Strategy
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 4 (LinkedIn section)
**Core Principle:** LinkedIn is the "library" or "boardroom" of social networks — users are in business/career mindset. Organic reach reminds GaryVee of Facebook in 2012 (high organic reach era). Gold mine for B2B, executives, service providers. Personal profiles get more organic reach than company pages. Build up HUMAN BEINGS at your company, not just the corporate brand. Creators who think they're "too cool" for LinkedIn are leaving opportunities on the table. Even consumer brands benefit: you're selling to HUMANS, and those humans are on LinkedIn.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: B2B lead generation, executive brand building, professional services, any business wanting to reach professionals
- AVOID WHEN: never — should be part of every professional's strategy
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:CT]`, `[DTA:CM]`, `[DTA:PA]`

### [DTA:LI.1] LinkedIn Personal vs. Company
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 4
**Core Principle:** Content from personal profiles gets SIGNIFICANTLY more organic distribution than company pages. Strategy: Build personal brands of executives and team members. When a user likes/comments on a LinkedIn post, it resurfaces in THEIR connections' and followers' feeds — unique amplification mechanism. Have 15-25 senior employees produce content instead of relying on one person.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Post from personal profiles AND company page (but prioritize personal)
2. Build personal brands of multiple executives (hedge against any one leaving)
3. When connections engage with your post, it resurfaces to THEIR network
4. Connect with target decision-makers directly, then create content they'd engage with
5. Their engagement distributes your post to their network of similar executives
6. B2B SaaS: 2-5 pieces of organic content/day minimum on LinkedIn
7. Run overperforming personal content as ads through the company page

### [DTA:LI.2] LinkedIn Content Approach
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 4
**Core Principle:** Treat LinkedIn as a content platform, not just a sales engine. Stop cold-pitching through DMs. Instead, create relevant content → build trust → run overperforming content as ads. Act like you're the "B2B magazine" of your industry — provide value, not promotion. Copy is EXTRA important on LinkedIn because users are in a reading/business mindset. Use long-form copy, articles, newsletters. Sprinkle in personal stories among professional content.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Act like the "B2B magazine" of your industry — give value, not sales pitches
2. Long, thorough copy — users are in reading mode
3. Use LinkedIn articles and newsletters as creative units
4. Sprinkle personal stories, hobbies, passions among professional content
5. Start a podcast → invite target decision-makers as guests → build relationships + content
6. Green screen videos over articles/press releases work well
7. LinkedIn polls for engagement and insights
8. In-app templates (colored background text posts) for easy creation
9. Memes work on LinkedIn too (Morning Brew example)
10. PDF uploads function as carousels (since native carousel removed June 2023)

### [DTA:LI.3] LinkedIn Ad Targeting
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 4
**Core Principle:** LinkedIn ads are more expensive than other platforms but offer unmatched B2B targeting: by job title, seniority, specific company, industry. If you're a B2B SaaS company, you can literally run ads to employees at the 50 specific companies you want to sell to, with creative contextualized to each organization. For B2B, a few hundred views from the right decision-makers is enough for ROI.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Target by job title, seniority, company name
2. Create unique email/phone number for LinkedIn lead gen (clear attribution)
3. Contextualize creative to specific organizations you're targeting
4. Run overperforming organic content as ads with sales CTA
5. B2B: Don't compare to B2C view counts — 10 engaged decision-makers can = multiple sales
6. Use organic insights to identify what messaging resonates before spending on ads

## Facebook

### [DTA:FB] Facebook Strategy
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 4 (Facebook section)
**Core Principle:** Facebook is one of the most UNDERESTIMATED platforms. ~3B MAU (largest platform in the book). Creators dismissing it as "mom's social media app" are leaving massive opportunity on the table. Facebook Reels have brought BACK organic reach after years of decline. Local businesses especially should pay attention — users are in a "hometown," "local" mindset when checking Facebook. The "Youthification of Society" means even older users behave more like younger consumers.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: local businesses, broad consumer reach, political campaigns, reaching 30+ demographics, any business that wants organic reach
- AVOID WHEN: never — should be part of every strategy
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:OR]`, `[DTA:PA]`, `[DTA:PN]`

### [DTA:FB.1] Facebook Reels & Organic Reach
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 4
**Core Principle:** Facebook went through years of declining organic reach as the platform matured. Now, with Facebook Reels and interest-graph algorithm development, organic reach is BACK. Businesses, creators, and influencers can reach millions of people who don't follow them. Facebook also has one of the best analytics suites (second to YouTube): audience retention, performance by hour, follower vs. nonfollower breakdown, benchmark vs. account average. Native A/B testing of organic content is a unique feature.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Post Facebook Reels consistently — organic reach is back
2. Use Facebook's native A/B test feature for organic content
3. Analyze: audience retention, followers vs. nonfollowers reached, performance vs. your average
4. Mix creative units: text posts, Reels, GIFs, notes, live streams
5. Facebook allows photo replies in comments — use CTAs to incentivize this
6. Local businesses: Run ads within mile radius of location — users in "hometown" mindset
7. Stop posting generic "good evening" photos — apply SOC and PAC principles

### [DTA:FB.2] Facebook Groups & Community
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 4
**Core Principle:** Posting in Facebook Groups is an overlooked opportunity for brand building. It's like going to a chamber of commerce event — bring value, don't spam. Creating or engaging in groups related to your industry builds relationships that lead to business.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Join Facebook Groups relevant to your industry/audience
2. Provide genuine value, not sales pitches
3. Consider creating your own group for your niche
4. Answer questions, share expertise, be the helpful expert
5. Use group conversations for content ideas and consumer insights

### [DTA:FB.3] Facebook for Local & Political
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 6 (Political scenario)
**Core Principle:** Facebook is GaryVee's #1 recommendation for local businesses and political campaigns. Users are in a "hometown" mindset. The platform allows hyper-local ad targeting. For political campaigns: go live for virtual "town halls," post at scale locally, run $20-30 ads sharing opinions with local area. The older demographic that votes more tends to be on Facebook.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Run ads targeting specific zip codes/mile radius
2. Facebook Live for town halls, Q&As, community engagement
3. Post local content referencing town-specific elements
4. Run $50-100 ads per piece of content in local area — very affordable for local reach
5. Film everything at local events → post clips → amplify winners
6. Political campaigns: Test 40-50 topics in organic posts → run $100 ads on each → build campaign platform from what resonates

## YouTube

### [DTA:YT] YouTube Strategy
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 4 (YouTube section)
**Core Principle:** YouTube is the "central entertainment hub" — people expect TV-like production and film-like content. The platform's Google DNA gives it the MOST advanced analytics of any platform. YouTube is the world's second-largest search engine — SEO-optimized titles make your content discoverable in both YouTube and Google search results. Nearly half of YouTube viewership happens on TV screens (connected TVs). Both long-form and Shorts work.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: building authority, long-form content, searchable how-to content, B2B education, brand storytelling
- AVOID WHEN: never — should be part of every strategy, even if just Shorts
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:PN]`, `[DTA:QQ]`, `[DTA:PA]`

### [DTA:YT.1] YouTube Analytics & Testing
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 4
**Core Principle:** YouTube's analytics are the most advanced of any platform: click-through rates on thumbnails, audience retention graphs, swipe-away rates on Shorts, long-form replay data. The most successful YouTubers (like Mr. Beast) obsess over analytics. Test everything: large text vs. small text, face on thumbnail vs. not, facial expressions, transparent vs. solid backgrounds. YouTube even lets you change thumbnails/titles and see the impact on views and CTR.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Study audience retention graphs — find where people drop off
2. A/B test thumbnails and titles (YouTube allows this)
3. Monitor click-through rate — optimize thumbnails based on data
4. Track Shorts swipe-away rate
5. Find which moments get most replays/shares
6. Lean into the "science side" of content on YouTube more than any other platform
7. Mr. Beast-level optimization: test every variable systematically

### [DTA:YT.2] YouTube Search & SEO
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 4
**Core Principle:** YouTube is the #2 search engine globally. Title your videos with SEO keywords. Content can show up in YouTube search AND Google search. Especially valuable for specialized services and B2B. A real estate agent making videos about "Living in Newton, MA" will show up when people search that term. This creates long-term value — content continues driving traffic months or years later.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Research keywords your target audience searches on YouTube
2. Include keywords in video title, description, and tags
3. Create how-to content around questions your audience asks
4. YouTube Shorts with SEO titles: distributed through Shorts feed AND searchable
5. B2B: How-to content on YouTube has long shelf life and search visibility
6. Local businesses: Include city/town names in video titles for local search visibility

### [DTA:YT.3] YouTube as Content Hub
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 4
**Core Principle:** YouTube is ideal for long-form content: podcasts, day-in-the-life vlogs, documentaries, live streams. Live streaming on YouTube is especially powerful: (1) Creates a live "show" experience, (2) After stream ends, you have a long-form video to retitle with SEO and add thumbnails. YouTube mobile lets users play audio in background while doing other things — like a podcast app. Connected TVs make YouTube the modern television.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Live stream podcasts/interviews on YouTube → becomes long-form video after
2. Retitle live streams with SEO keywords + add optimized thumbnail after stream ends
3. Long-form "day in the life" or vlog content fits YouTube's psychology
4. Chop long-form into Shorts for the Shorts feed
5. YouTube Community tab for text posts, polls, engagement
6. Use YouTube pre-roll ads for "modern commercials" that people actually want to watch
7. YouTube → gateway to CTV/connected TV ads

## Snapchat

### [DTA:SC] Snapchat Strategy (Note: tag SC used to avoid conflict — see citation table)
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 4 (Snapchat section)
**Core Principle:** Snapchat is different from other platforms: hasn't aged up as much, used more for messaging than content discovery, skews youngest. Spotlight (TikTok-like feed) and Discover (publishers/influencers) are the main content discovery features. Wide variations in content performance on Spotlight — a sign of early maturity as content platform = opportunity. Snapchat has advanced AR tools. Local content is core to Snapchat's DNA (location sharing on map). Keep it as a "side dish" — your main platforms + Snapchat presence so you're ready if/when it grows.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: reaching youngest demographics, local business advertising, AR marketing, maintaining presence for future opportunity
- AVOID WHEN: B2B insurance or similarly older-demographic businesses (keep a minimal presence but don't prioritize)
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:PN]`, `[DTA:TF]`
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Maintain a presence even if secondary — be ready if it ages up
2. Adapt TikTok content with Snapchat-native elements (in-app text bars, etc.)
3. Snapchat content should skew younger than TikTok content
4. Local businesses: Run ads within small radius — local DNA is strong
5. Explore AR lens creation tools — Snapchat leads in AR
6. Wide performance variations on Spotlight = early opportunity signal
7. "Side dish" strategy: Post adapted content from other platforms, stay active, don't over-invest

## Emerging & Secondary Platforms

### Pinterest, Threads, BeReal, WhatsApp
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Conclusion + Part 6
**Core Principle:** Always maintain awareness of emerging platforms and secondary ones. Pinterest, Threads (Meta's X competitor), and WhatsApp as marketing channel are all on GaryVee's radar. Some of the biggest growth arbitrages happen on platforms outside the top 5 — the dozen people who take a secondary platform seriously find disproportionate opportunity. The key principle: be wherever people's eyes and ears are.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Threads: Underestimated by most → opportunity for early movers
2. Pinterest: Often overlooked but has real attention and purchasing intent
3. WhatsApp: Emerging marketing channel, especially globally
4. BeReal: Monitor for future relevance
5. Decentralized social networks: Keep an eye on blockchain-based platforms
6. AI translation: Use AI to translate content into multiple languages for global reach
7. Any new platform: Even if it doesn't last, the learnings transfer and the brand value you captured remains

## Platform-Specific Ad Strategies

### [DTA:PA] Paid Amplification by Platform

**Facebook/Instagram Ads** `[DTA:FB]` `[DTA:IG]`:
- Advantage+ Shopping Campaigns: Feed up to 150 creative variations, algorithm optimizes
- Broad targeting + trust algorithm — creative IS the targeting
- Narrow targeting for hyper-local businesses (mile radius)
- Meta leads in algorithm sophistication for performance ads

**TikTok Ads** `[DTA:TK]`:
- Don't dismiss because it "doesn't convert" — same was said about Facebook in 2011
- Shop Retargeting: Retarget people who interacted with TikTok Shop
- In-app shopping: TikTok building commerce ecosystem
- Conversion capabilities improving rapidly

**YouTube/Google Ads** `[DTA:YT]`:
- Performance Max: Optimizes across YouTube, Display, Search, Discover, Gmail, Maps
- Pre-roll ads for "modern commercials" people actually want to watch
- YouTube → gateway to CTV/connected TV ads (Roku, Fire TV)
- YouTube + Shopify: Users can buy through TV remote

**LinkedIn Ads** `[DTA:LI]`:
- Most expensive but most precise B2B targeting
- Target by job title, seniority, specific company
- Run overperforming organic content as ads with lead-gen CTA
- Create unique tracking (email/phone) for clear attribution

**Streaming/CTV Ads**:
- Hulu: Small businesses can run ads from $500/month
- Roku + Instacart: CPG brands can track if people bought after seeing ad
- Roku + Shopify: Buy products through TV remote
- FAST channels (Tubi, Pluto TV): More accessible ad inventory
- Use social media insights to de-risk higher-cost CTV creative

**Influencer Marketing** `[DTA:PA]`:
- Micro-influencers > celebrity endorsements for most businesses
- Send product samples to emerging creators on TikTok — interest graph can amplify beyond follower count
- "Alpha moms": Local micro-influencers with strong community influence (even 2K followers)
- Invite local influencers for in-store events/meet-and-greets
- Film everything at influencer events for content
- Let influencers BE AUTHENTIC — don't micromanage their content output
- TikTok Creator Marketplace, YouTube BrandConnect for finding partners
