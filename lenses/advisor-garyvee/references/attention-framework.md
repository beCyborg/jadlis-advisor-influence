# Day Trading Attention — Core Framework

## Foundation: The Attention Trading Model

### [DTA:AT] Attention Trading (Core Metaphor)
**Source:** Day Trading Attention (GaryVee), Introduction + Conclusion
**Core Principle:** Attention is an asset, like real estate. Just as a day trader studies financial markets daily to find underpriced stocks, marketers must constantly study where human attention sits and where it is underpriced relative to the cost of reaching people there. Day trading attention means identifying what people are paying attention to, the cost of capturing that attention, and how it shifts by the day.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: choosing marketing channels, allocating budget, deciding which platforms to invest time in, evaluating new opportunities
- AVOID WHEN: you have zero content creation capability (fix that first), you're spreading so thin across platforms that quality drops to zero
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:OR]`, `[DTA:PN]`, `[DTA:PA]`
**Key Examples:**
- GaryVee bought wine terms on Google AdWords for 10 cents/click in the early 2000s, built Wine Library business primarily through underpriced email marketing, Google AdWords, and YouTube
- Malibu real estate analogy: 50 years ago, buying beachfront property before prices rose = buying attention on platforms before ad costs rise
- Super Bowl commercials are $7M for 30 seconds but are "a bargain" because 115M people actually watch the ads — attention is underpriced relative to cost
- TV commercials at $100K+ are generally overpriced because attention has declined but costs haven't adjusted
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Study where mass consumer attention currently sits (social platforms, streaming, etc.)
2. Evaluate the cost of storytelling on each platform (organic = zero cost, paid = varies)
3. Identify the supply-demand mismatch: fewer content producers + lots of attention = underpriced
4. Go all in on underpriced attention WHILE learning the craft of storytelling there
5. Continuously reassess — what's underpriced today won't be forever
**Reversal:** Even underpriced attention is worthless if your content/execution is bad. A $7M Super Bowl spot is wasted if the ad is boring. The medium alone doesn't guarantee results — you must be good at storytelling within that medium.

### [DTA:AT.1] Supply and Demand of Content
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Introduction
**Core Principle:** When there's lots of attention on a platform but few content producers, you have disproportionate opportunity. As more creators flood in, standing out requires more strategy. Early adopters capture massive organic reach; latecomers must work harder. This cycle repeats with every platform and every new feature within platforms.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: evaluating whether to join a new platform, feature, or creative format; deciding urgency of action
- AVOID WHEN: you're only joining platforms for FOMO without committing to real content production
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:AT]`, `[DTA:TF]`, `[DTA:OR]`
**Key Examples:**
- YouTube Shorts 2021-22: GaryVee saw greatest spikes in views when Shorts first launched (few creators, lots of viewers)
- Instagram DMs 2015-16: People made biggest business deals through DMs because celebrities and executives were directly reachable
- TikTok 2019-20: Organic reach was "obnoxiously high"; by 2023 still high but requires better content
- Wine Library email marketing 1990s: 91% open rates on 100K-person list — impossible to replicate today
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Identify platforms/features in early maturity (high attention, low content supply)
2. Move FAST — the window of easiest organic reach closes as more creators join
3. Don't dismiss an opportunity because it's "not as good as it was" — even at higher competition, it may still be underpriced
4. Watch for new features WITHIN platforms (e.g., YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, LinkedIn newsletters) — same supply-demand dynamics apply
5. The progression: entrepreneurs/creators move first → small businesses → big brands → opportunity evens out
**Reversal:** Even on established platforms, the opportunity isn't dead — GaryVee thought Google AdWords at $1/click was "over" when it rose from 10 cents; in hindsight, even $1 was cheap. Don't abandon a platform just because the peak arbitrage window has passed.

### [DTA:AT.2] Attention as Real Estate
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Introduction
**Core Principle:** Different advertising platforms are like different real estate markets — some are overpriced, some underpriced. Television commercials are generally overpriced (high cost, declining attention). Social media organic reach is underpriced (zero cost, massive attention). The skill is recognizing the mismatch and acting on it.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: allocating marketing budget between channels, comparing ROI across platforms
- AVOID WHEN: you have zero budget and should focus exclusively on organic before even considering paid
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:AT]`, `[DTA:PA]`
**Key Examples:**
- TV commercials: $100K+ but declining viewership (2022 Winter Olympics had lowest-ever US ratings)
- Facebook: ~3 billion MAU, organic reach = literally zero cost
- TikTok: Still massive organic reach even in 2023/2024
- Super Bowl: The one exception to "TV is overpriced" — 115M viewers who actually watch ads = best deal in US advertising
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Map all channels you're using or considering
2. For each: What's the cost? What's the actual attention (not "potential reach")?
3. Calculate cost-per-actual-attention, not just CPM
4. Reallocate budget from overpriced channels to underpriced ones
5. Exception: No channel is ever completely "dead" — radio, print, billboards can work if priced correctly and executed well
**Reversal:** Even overpriced channels can work if execution is excellent and the buy is strategic. Don't write anything off entirely — the question is always whether the price is right relative to the attention you'll get.

## The TikTokification Paradigm

### [DTA:TF] TikTokification of Social Media
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 1
**Core Principle:** All platforms are shifting from social graph (content based on who you follow) to interest graph (content based on what you're interested in). TikTok's For You page pioneered this, and now Instagram (Reels/Explore), YouTube (Shorts/recommendations), X (For You feed), Facebook (Reels), Snapchat (Spotlight), and LinkedIn are all copying this model. This is the defining shift of modern social media.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: creating content strategy, deciding whether follower count matters, evaluating content performance
- AVOID WHEN: never — this principle is always relevant in 2024+
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:OR]`, `[DTA:QQ]`, `[DTA:HK]`, `[DTA:CT]`
**Key Examples:**
- GaryVee with millions of followers: a bad piece of content might get only a few thousand views; a car dealership with 15 followers might get millions of views with a relevant video
- Tumblr was the first "interest-based" platform GaryVee invested in (before TikTok): "On Tumblr, you follow things you're interested in"
- Musical.ly (2014, acquired by ByteDance 2017, merged into TikTok 2018): first platform GaryVee saw that distributed content based on interests, not followers
- Interest-based communities on TikTok: #booktok, #corporatetok, #sportstok, #winetok — communities formed around genres, not personalities
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Stop obsessing over follower count — creative quality is the variable of success
2. Make content that's RELEVANT to specific groups of people, not generic content for everyone
3. Expect wide variability: 100 views → 10K → 300 → 500K on successive posts. This is NORMAL in interest-graph world
4. Use this to your advantage: every piece of content is a fresh chance at distribution, regardless of past performance
5. Test hypotheses through content: post it and see what the algorithm distributes it to, then refine
**Reversal:** Followers still matter somewhat — some platforms still weight follower relationships more than others. LinkedIn, for example, hasn't advanced its interest graph as far as TikTok. But the trend is universal and accelerating.

### [DTA:TF.1] Interest Graph vs. Social Graph
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 1
**Core Principle:** Social graph = content from people you follow. Interest graph = content based on what you're interested in, regardless of who you follow. The interest graph creates more relevant feeds, keeps users on platforms longer, and allows new creators/businesses to reach audiences from day zero. Platforms are incentivized to distribute GOOD content because they compete with each other for user attention.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: deciding whether to invest in building followers vs. investing in content quality
- AVOID WHEN: never — always relevant
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:TF]`, `[DTA:OR]`, `[DTA:QQ]`
**Key Examples:**
- Facebook's original EdgeRank algorithm (2009): analyzed interactions to decide what to show, but still within social graph container
- TikTok's For You page: considers watch time, likes, shares, comments, content you create, device type, location, language — all processed by recommendation engine, weighted per user
- Prior to TikTokification: marketing on social was like email marketing — build a list, market to that list. Now you don't need a follower base first
- Doggface's Ocean Spray video: regular guy with no following → 90M views because the interest graph found the right audiences
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Content is now the variable of success, not followers
2. Your content competes in the user's feed against ALL content, not just content from accounts they follow
3. Make content so good that the platform WANTS to distribute it (platforms compete for user attention — they reward good content)
4. Create content for specific interest communities, not just "everyone"
5. Monitor what percentage of your views come from followers vs. non-followers — most platforms show this in analytics
**Reversal:** On platforms like LinkedIn (as of 2024), the interest graph is less advanced — connections and followers still matter more than on TikTok. Adjust strategy per platform.

### [DTA:TF.2] Creative Is the Variable of Success
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Parts 1-3
**Core Principle:** In the interest-graph era, the quality and relevance of your content determines your reach, not your follower count, your ad budget, or your brand size. Small businesses compete on equal footing with Fortune 500 brands. The platform just wants to keep users on longer — if your content does that, you're rewarded. This is the most merit-based system in advertising history.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: deciding where to invest — content quality vs. ad spend vs. follower growth
- AVOID WHEN: you're running purely direct-response ads where targeting and funnel matter more than creative
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:HK]`, `[DTA:QQ]`, `[DTA:PN]`, `[DTA:CT]`
**Key Examples:**
- Logan Paul & KSI's Prime Hydration competing with major beverage brands
- Chamberlain Coffee (Emma Chamberlain) competing with Folgers
- Mr. Beast's Feastables competing with largest consumer brands
- Dollar Shave Club: $4,500 video → 12,000 orders in 48 hours
- Cement contractors getting big deals through TikTok content
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Invest disproportionately in content quality and relevance, not just ad spend
2. Study what "quality" means on each platform — it's NOT production value, it's relevance and engagement
3. Test constantly — you can't predict what creative will work; volume gives you data
4. Even with zero budget, compelling content can reach millions
5. The creative is more important than the media buy — a $7M Super Bowl spot is wasted on a boring ad
**Reversal:** For purely conversion-focused campaigns (especially retargeting), technical targeting and funnel optimization still matter a lot. Creative is THE variable for reach and brand building, but not the ONLY variable in a complete marketing strategy.

## Organic Reach

### [DTA:OR] Organic Reach
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Introduction + Parts 1-3
**Core Principle:** Social media platforms offer literally zero-cost organic reach — the number of people who see your content if you just post it, without running ads. This is historically unprecedented in advertising. No billboard, TV commercial, or print ad has ever offered free distribution based on content quality. In the interest-graph era, organic reach is virtually unlimited for content that's relevant enough.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: you have more time than money, you want to build brand, you want to test content before amplifying with paid
- AVOID WHEN: you need immediate, predictable reach in a specific geographic area (use paid), you're selling to hyper-niche B2B audience (supplement with LinkedIn ads)
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:PA]`, `[DTA:QQ]`, `[DTA:TF]`, `[DTA:PN]`
**Key Examples:**
- Facebook: ~3B MAU, zero cost to post organically
- Instagram & YouTube: ~2B each
- TikTok: massive organic reach even in 2024, though not as extreme as 2019-20
- Doggface/Ocean Spray: zero-cost organic video → 90M views, massive sales impact, Fleetwood Mac "Dreams" back to #1 on iTunes
- TrapStix lip balm: 75 orders in 2 years, then TikTok videos → 50K units in 2 years
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Organic content is your foundation — build this FIRST before spending on ads
2. Post at high volume across multiple platforms to maximize organic exposure
3. Use organic performance as R&D: identify what content resonates before amplifying with paid
4. Don't dismiss a platform because organic reach has "declined" — it's still free and still works if content is good
5. More relevance = more organic reach. The platform rewards relevant content with distribution
**Reversal:** Organic reach alone may not be sufficient for businesses that need immediate, targeted results (e.g., local plumber needs calls THIS month). In these cases, combine organic with targeted paid amplification. Also, organic reach on company pages (vs. personal profiles) tends to be lower on platforms like LinkedIn.

### [DTA:OR.1] Marketing for the Sake of Better Marketing
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Parts 1-3
**Core Principle:** Strategic organic content is not "test and learn" or "spray and pray." It's actual marketing that builds brand AND generates insights for future marketing. Every piece of organic content is simultaneously: (1) brand-building advertising, (2) consumer research, and (3) a potential ad creative to amplify. This is fundamentally different from the old model where you'd do research → make an expensive ad → hope it works.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: planning content strategy, justifying high-volume content production, responding to "why should we post so much?"
- AVOID WHEN: you're treating each post as a one-off "test" instead of a brand-building exercise
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:QQ]`, `[DTA:PA]`, `[DTA:CM]`
**Key Examples:**
- Stand-up comedians: try material at small comedy clubs → refine based on reactions → Netflix special has very little guessing
- GEICO: could use social content to test Martin the Gecko vs. Caveman before committing to one in big campaigns
- Athletic clothing brand: make 20-40+ pieces of content for narrow demographics of moms → see what works → build TV commercials based on proven concepts
- Starbucks tweet about "showing up late with coffee" → could seed a whole ad campaign
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Post organic content daily as brand-building exercises (not "tests")
2. Track what overperforms your average — these are proven concepts
3. Use overperforming organic content as the basis for paid ads (tweak with sales elements)
4. Use learnings to inform higher-production "modern commercials"
5. Read every comment for qualitative insights on what consumers care about
**Reversal:** Don't use "marketing for better marketing" as an excuse to never do direct-response or sales-focused content. The pipeline must eventually lead to actual sales asks.

## Platform-Native Content

### [DTA:PN] Platform-Native Content
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 3 (SOC section) + Part 4
**Core Principle:** Each platform is a different environment with different context, features, and user psychology. Just as you act differently in a boardroom vs. a trip to Vegas with friends, your content must adapt to each platform. "Native" content blends into the platform — it uses in-app features, matches the tone, references platform-specific behaviors, and doesn't feel like it was lazily cross-posted from somewhere else.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: creating content for any platform, repurposing content across platforms, deciding how to adapt a single idea for multiple channels
- AVOID WHEN: you have zero resources and must choose between platform-native content on one platform vs. identical content on five (choose quality over reach in extreme resource constraints)
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:RP]`, `[DTA:HK]`, `[DTA:CT]`
**Key Examples:**
- Chipotle: Same video on TikTok and Instagram Reels but with DIFFERENT trending sounds native to each platform
- Snapchat text bars: Unique in-app feature for adding text — using it makes content feel native to Snapchat
- Instagram carousels vs. TikTok carousels: Different behaviors (swiping past last TikTok carousel slide leads to profile; Instagram doesn't)
- McDonald's: Same message on Facebook (text post) and Instagram (Twitter-style graphic sized for Instagram)
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Use each platform's in-app features (TikTok effects, Instagram polls, Snapchat lenses, LinkedIn templates)
2. Adapt copy to reference platform-specific actions ("save this post" on IG, "bookmark this" on X, "send to a colleague" on LinkedIn)
3. Match the tone of the platform (TikTok = lighthearted, LinkedIn = professional/career, Facebook = local/hometown feel)
4. Adjust dimensions and format (Instagram grid thumbnails, YouTube SEO titles, etc.)
5. Use platform-native audio — trending sounds on TikTok are different from Instagram Reels
6. Even small tweaks make a difference: changing just the copy or audio = better than identical cross-posting
**Reversal:** If you're at absolute zero content output, cross-posting identical content is better than posting nothing. But know that platform-native content will always outperform lazy repurposing.

### [DTA:PN.1] Platform User Psychology
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 3 (PAC section) + Part 4
**Core Principle:** Every platform has its own psychological context. Users are in different mindsets depending on which app they're using. LinkedIn = business/career mindset. Facebook = seeing what high school friends are up to, "hometown" feel. TikTok = lighthearted, casual. YouTube = TV-like entertainment, higher production expected. Snapchat = intimate, messaging-first, young. Your content must match the mental state of users on each platform.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: ideating content, writing copy, choosing topics, deciding production level
- AVOID WHEN: never — this is always relevant for platform strategy
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:PN]`, `[DTA:CT]`, `[DTA:HK]`
**Key Examples:**
- LinkedIn comments: When a user likes/comments, it resurfaces in their connections' feeds — design content to generate business discussions
- Facebook: "Hometown" and "local" mindset — great for local businesses, political campaigns
- TikTok: Lighthearted — use in-app fonts, trending audio, casual tone even for serious topics
- YouTube: "Central entertainment hub" — people expect higher production, documentaries, longer formats
- Snapchat: Skews youngest, intimate messaging platform first — content platform second
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Ask: "What mindset is the user in when they open this app?"
2. Match your content's tone, format, and topic to that mindset
3. LinkedIn: Sprinkle personal stories among professional content to stand out
4. Facebook: Lean into local, community, and nostalgic elements
5. TikTok: Keep it casual even for B2B or technical content — use in-app creative features
6. YouTube: Invest more in production, thumbnails, and SEO
7. Snapchat: Use native features like AR lenses, keep it young and casual
**Reversal:** Breaking platform conventions deliberately CAN work if done authentically — e.g., a very personal/vulnerable post on LinkedIn might overperform BECAUSE it breaks the professional norm. But know the rules before you break them.

## Quantity + Quality

### [DTA:QQ] Quantity + Quality (Not Either/Or)
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 3 (SOC section)
**Core Principle:** The "quality vs. quantity" debate is a false dichotomy. The answer is BOTH. But quantity is what gives you an indication of what quality actually is. The more formats you try, the more consumer segments you target, the more insights you get on what resonates. Don't wait for the perfect piece of content — post 4-5 times per day across platforms to maximize your chances of having something hit. Don't judge your strategy until you've posted consistently for 50-100 days.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: someone says "I'm focusing on quality over quantity," or when content output is less than 1 post/day, or when someone is overthinking content before posting
- AVOID WHEN: someone is already posting 5+/day and needs to focus on strategic improvements to what they're posting
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:OR]`, `[DTA:TF]`, `[DTA:CT]`, `[DTA:RP]`
**Key Examples:**
- VeeCon 2023: Two partners wanted to "change the world" but were publishing one post a week. GaryVee filmed content with them on the spot (garyvee.com/quitoverthinking)
- Per-post vs. total reach: posting 4x/day might drop from 1,000 views/post to 500/post, but total = 2,000/day vs. 1,000/day — net gain
- GaryVee posts about garage sales, business, mindset, VeeFriends, NY Jets, blueberries — a video about blueberries was one of his most successful ever
- "You can't read about doing push-ups and get in shape"
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Set a daily minimum post count (aim for 4-5 across each platform)
2. Don't wait for the "lightning bolt" — one perfect post is less effective than many good ones
3. Vary formats: video, carousel, image, text, live, stories — different formats reach different people
4. Vary topics: show different sides of yourself/business — don't stick to one lane forever (80/20 rule: 80% your core, 20% something different)
5. Judge strategy after 50-100 days of consistent posting, not after 4 posts
6. You're NOT "oversaturating" the feed — most of your content isn't even being seen
**Reversal:** Quantity without ANY strategy is less effective. Don't post 20 identical, low-effort posts/day. The point is: quantity FUELS quality discovery — each post is a data point. Also, on some platforms like YouTube long-form, one excellent video/week may be better than 5 mediocre ones.

### [DTA:QQ.1] The Lightning Bolt Model (Anti-Pattern)
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 3
**Core Principle:** Don't spend all your time and resources trying to get one piece of content "right" and hoping to strike gold. Instead, put out 4-5 posts across platforms every day to give yourself more chances. Brands especially make this mistake — spending months on one "perfect" campaign instead of producing volume and learning from the market. SOC is "marketing for the sake of better marketing" — you build brand with every post AND learn what works.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: someone is paralyzed trying to create the "perfect" post, or a brand has been in planning mode for weeks/months without publishing
- AVOID WHEN: someone needs to slow down and be more strategic about what they're posting (rare — most people need to post MORE)
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:QQ]`, `[DTA:OR]`
**Key Examples:**
- Fortune 500 brands: spend months defining guidelines, doing focus groups, selling ideas internally → one generic 30-second TV commercial. Instead, could post 20-40 organic videos, see what works, THEN make the commercial
- "Hold off on saying your strategy isn't working until you've posted for 50-100 days"
- Tennis analogy: practicing 5 days/week improves your game more than once/week
- Working out analogy: "When you first start working out, maybe you just want to get your stamina up. As you keep going, you start doing more challenging workouts"
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Stop planning, start posting — the first post doesn't need to be perfect
2. Set a production system: record long-form content (e.g., podcast) → chop into clips for multiple platforms
3. Use "rule of 2 minutes": before planning a content session, create something for 2 minutes first
4. Every piece of content you post teaches you something — embrace this learning loop
5. Done is better than perfect. Posted beats polished-but-never-published every time
**Reversal:** This doesn't mean abandon all strategy — you should still define cohorts, understand platforms, and have a POV. But don't let strategy become a substitute for execution.

## Hooks and Retention

### [DTA:HK] Hooks & Retention
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 3 (SOC section)
**Core Principle:** The hook is the first thing users see when they encounter your content — it dictates the first impression and determines whether they scroll past or stop. For video: everything in the first 3 seconds (title, thumbnail, opening line, character, design). For images: colors, text, layout. The hook is where the audience decides if your content is relevant to them. In the attention economy, you compete against obnoxious amounts of content — if you don't catch eyes in 1-2 seconds, they're gone.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: creating any content for any platform (always relevant), diagnosing why content is underperforming, optimizing existing content
- AVOID WHEN: never — hooks always matter
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:PN]`, `[DTA:CT]`, `[DTA:QQ]`
**Key Examples:**
- Physical therapist video: Title "Your body after 30" + green screen format + Mission Impossible audio → 26.5M views. Title directly calls out a cohort (people over 30)
- Fox Financial street interview: Opens with "I've got 5 questions, get them all right, win the prize" with "$500" text → dramatically outperformed their other videos
- Stellar Sidewalks: "I wish more DIYers knew..." + satisfying visual → millions of views on a CONCRETE company's TikTok
- Instagram carousels: First tile needs a hint that there's more to swipe through
**Actionable Framework:**
1. **First 3 seconds of video**: Title, opening line spoken aloud, camera angle/movement, character who looks like your target audience
2. **Titles that work**: Direct cohort callouts ("For all the dads out there"), curiosity ("Where is the surgeon hiding?"), strong POV, "Did you know" format
3. **Eliminate fluff**: No slow intros, no extra frames, no pauses. Get into the message IMMEDIATELY
4. **Readability**: Use in-app text features with contrasting backgrounds. White text on green might not work — test multiple color combinations
5. **Thumbnail strategy**: Different from the hook but equally important on YouTube and platform grids. Test different thumbnails for the same video
6. **Audio hook**: Use trending/familiar audio to catch attention (Mission Impossible theme, etc.)
7. **Repost with changes**: Try same content with different hooks — different title, different opening, different format — post both and compare
**Reversal:** A great hook that leads to boring content still fails. The hook gets them to stop; the content keeps them watching. Both matter. Also, clickbait hooks that don't deliver on their promise will hurt you long-term through lower watch time and potentially less algorithmic favor.

### [DTA:HK.1] First Three Seconds Rule
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 3 + Part 4 (TikTok section)
**Core Principle:** The first three seconds of a video are the most critical factor in distribution, especially on TikTok. This is when the algorithm evaluates whether users want to watch and when users decide if the content is relevant to them. Every element must work in these three seconds: title text, spoken words, camera angle, character featured, and visual movement. On TikTok specifically, this is one of the biggest factors behind how widely your video gets distributed in the For You page.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: creating any video content for any platform, especially TikTok and Instagram Reels
- AVOID WHEN: creating long-form YouTube content where the first 30 seconds + thumbnail matter more than first 3 seconds specifically
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:HK]`, `[DTA:TF]`, `[DTA:PN]`
**Key Examples:**
- "Car Dealership Red Flags, Part 1": Large title + person saying it aloud + educational skit format → 645K likes
- Green screen duet format: Creator's face in corner + video playing in background → curiosity about what creator will say
- "POV: you're a girl dad": Title in first seconds that calls out specific cohort → immediately signals relevance
- Stellar Sidewalks: "I wish more DIYers knew..." immediately calls out cohort + creates curiosity
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Add title text visible in frame 1 — don't wait to type it on screen
2. Start speaking immediately — no "hey guys, welcome to my channel" warmups
3. Call out your cohort directly in the first line/title
4. Use camera movement or visual change in first second to create visual interest
5. Feature people who LOOK LIKE your target audience in the opening frames
6. Make the opening more broadly relatable while the rest can be narrow to your topic
7. A/B test: same video with two different first-3-second hooks, post both, compare
**Reversal:** On YouTube long-form, the first 30 seconds matter, but it's more about the thumbnail + title combo that gets the click. The "first 3 seconds" rule is most intense on short-form vertical video platforms.

## Content Pillars and Cohort Strategy

### [DTA:CT] Content Pillars & Cohort Development
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 2 + Part 3 (Cohort Development section)
**Core Principle:** Before creating content, define WHO you're making it for. Cohorts are narrow consumer segmentations — not "18-35 males" but "18-22 male Knicks fans in New York." The more specific your cohorts, the more relevant your content, the more the algorithm distributes it to the right people. Think of cohorts like an "accordion" — they expand, shrink, and change based on insights from your content performance and comments.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: planning content strategy, defining target audiences, deciding what content to create, experiencing declining reach
- AVOID WHEN: you're using cohort definition as an excuse to not start posting (don't over-plan before acting)
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:HK]`, `[DTA:CM]`, `[DTA:QQ]`
**Key Examples:**
- Kids clothing brand: Instead of "moms," define cohorts like "millennial moms who recently had first kid" and "girl dads" — each gets different content
- Nike: Some buy for street culture, some for Virgil Abloh, some for outlet deals on sweatpants — different meanings for different people
- Card trick creator: If your audience is young males but you want to sell out Vegas, create cohorts like "40-46-year-old moms in Chicago" to broaden appeal
- CPG brand: Break down by specific store locations + demographics in each area
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Define cohorts with "teeth" — specific age + location + interest/behavior combinations
2. Create a HIGH VOLUME of cohorts (20, 30, 40+), not just a handful of broad ones
3. Use the "accordion" model: Add/remove/adjust cohorts based on content performance and comments
4. Consider business objectives: Double down on segments that buy, expand into segments you're missing
5. Don't be crippled if content hits multiple cohorts — cohort labels are hypotheses to refine
6. Consider media addressability for future paid amplification, but don't overthink it — trust the algorithm to find the right people
7. Think beyond demographics: psychographics, life stages, subcultures, passions, cultural trends
**Reversal:** Going TOO narrow can limit your total addressable audience. Balance narrow cohorts (for relevance) with occasional broadly relatable content (for reach). Also, for B2B companies, even a few hundred views from the RIGHT people (decision-makers) is enough ROI.

### [DTA:CT.1] Boardroom Centric vs. Consumer Centric
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 3 (SOC section)
**Core Principle:** The biggest issue facing major brands is making content that subjectively fits their "brand guidelines" instead of what consumers actually want to see. Strict rules about fonts, colors, logo placement, product use cases, and "on brand" content make the content less interesting and less relevant. Brands should think about conveying different messages to different people instead of forcing the same format everywhere. "Consumer centric" beats "boardroom centric" every time.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: brand guidelines are limiting content performance, when someone says "that's not on brand", when discussing content strategy at companies with strict brand rules
- AVOID WHEN: the brand genuinely IS being damaged by off-brand content (rare), or when truly polarizing content is being created
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:CT]`, `[DTA:PN]`, `[DTA:QQ]`
**Key Examples:**
- Luxury brands: Won't do casual content even though streetwear collaborations broadened their appeal dramatically
- Athletic Greens: Same thumbnail design across videos — consistent brand but limits performance variety
- Fortune 500 brands: "pass off overproduced television ads as social media content"
- Ryanair: Self-deprecating "top 5 icks about Ryanair" — humanized the brand, stood out from competitors
- Duolingo: Mascot content completely dominates and helps grow actual business
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Challenge "brand guidelines" when they limit content relevance
2. Make content for the CONSUMER, not for internal stakeholders
3. Allow different fonts, colors, thumbnails, product use cases across content
4. Luxury brands: occasional casual content makes you more approachable without compromising brand value
5. B2B brands: Stop treating LinkedIn like a PR channel — act like the "B2B magazine" of your industry
6. Ask: "Would I want to watch this if I weren't employed by this company?"
**Reversal:** There IS a line — some brand protection is valid, especially for luxury brands dealing with dilution. The argument is not "no guidelines" but "fewer guidelines that get in the way of relevance." Also, authenticity matters — don't force casualness if it's not genuine.

## Repurposing

### [DTA:RP] Repurposing
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 3 (SOC section) + Part 5
**Core Principle:** One core piece of content can be distributed across multiple platforms with contextual tweaks. This is NOT lazy copy-pasting — it requires adapting the content to each platform's native format, tone, features, and audience psychology. A podcast interview becomes YouTube long-form + YouTube Shorts clips + Instagram Reels + TikToks + LinkedIn text posts + X/Twitter threads. GaryVee doesn't love blind cross-posting, but strategic repurposing with contextual tweaks multiplies your content output efficiently.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: you need to produce high-volume content efficiently, you have a podcast or long-form content to distribute, you want to maximize value from production shoots
- AVOID WHEN: you're using it as an excuse to copy-paste identical content across all platforms without ANY adaptation
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:PN]`, `[DTA:QQ]`, `[DTA:CT]`
**Key Examples:**
- Podcast → long-form YouTube video → Shorts clips → Instagram Reels → TikToks → LinkedIn text posts → X/Twitter threads
- McDonald's: Same message on Facebook (text post format) vs. Instagram (Twitter-style graphic)
- Deion "Coach Prime" Sanders: X/Twitter post → cropped screenshot → Instagram image
- "Rolling Stones Rule": Repost content that resonated again and again — McDonald's posted same message 2 years apart, still worked
- Production shoots "sawdust": While doing main shoot, capture behind-the-scenes, quick interviews, B-roll for 50+ additional content pieces
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Start with long-form: Record a podcast, film an event, do a Q&A session — this becomes your "content tree"
2. Chop into clips optimized for each platform (different lengths, different hooks, different copy)
3. Change audio/trending sounds per platform (what's trending on TikTok ≠ Instagram)
4. Adapt copy: "Share with a friend" (IG) vs. "Send to a colleague" (LinkedIn) vs. "Bookmark this" (X)
5. Use platform-native creative features to add titles, effects, polls
6. "Rolling Stones Rule": Great content can be reposted months or years later and still hit
7. From production shoots: capture the "sawdust" — behind-the-scenes, interviews, alternative angles = 50+ extra content pieces
**Reversal:** A video made SPECIFICALLY for Facebook will always outperform something repurposed from TikTok. Repurposing is a scaling strategy, not a substitute for platform-specific creation. When you can, create natively for each platform.

## Community & Comments

### [DTA:CM] Community & Comments
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 3 (PCS section)
**Core Principle:** Post-Creative Strategy (PCS) is about reading every comment on every post to extract consumer insights. Comments reveal what resonates, what doesn't, who your content is reaching, and what to create next. Comments are also a form of CONTENT — on TikTok, a comment reply can get more engagement than your videos. On LinkedIn, replying to comments resurfaces the post in followers' feeds. The "$1.80 strategy": leave your two cents on 90 posts/day to build awareness through comments.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: deciding what content to create next, seeking to understand audience, wanting to boost engagement and reach, building community
- AVOID WHEN: never — reading and engaging comments should be constant
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:CT]`, `[DTA:QQ]`, `[DTA:OR]`
**Key Examples:**
- Car dealership TikTok: comment reply got 2.8K likes — more than some of their other videos
- Wendy's: Built entire brand reputation on snarky comment replies
- GaryVee "$1.80 strategy" (2017): Leave thoughtful comments on 90 posts/day — still relevant in 2024
- LinkedIn: Replying to comments resurfaces the ENTIRE post to connections' feeds again
- Matt Higgins on LinkedIn: Didn't just reply "I agree" — expanded on the commenter's point, adding value
- VaynerMedia role "post-creative strategist": Primary job is reading every comment and extracting insights — like "anthropologists" or "psychologists"
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Read EVERY comment on every post you publish
2. Look for: What struck a nerve? What language do people use? What inside jokes? What unexpected audiences?
3. Use comment insights to add/remove/adjust cohorts (accordion model)
4. Reply to comments thoughtfully — not just "thanks!" but expand on what they said
5. Use video replies on TikTok to build depth with commenters
6. Leave thoughtful comments on OTHER accounts' posts in your industry for awareness
7. On LinkedIn: reply to comments to resurface your posts in followers' feeds
8. Pin best comments (Instagram allows up to 3 pinned at time of writing)
9. Use comments to spark content ideas: "Car dealership red flags" was a popular search term → content series
**Reversal:** Don't get so absorbed in comments that you stop creating new content. Comments inform strategy, but production must continue. Also, don't confuse generic positive comments ("love this!") with real insights — dig deeper for actionable patterns.

## Paid Amplification

### [DTA:PA] Paid Amplification
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 3 (Amplification section)
**Core Principle:** Amplification means spending against what ALREADY works. Don't run paid ads on content that hasn't been validated organically first. Post organic content → find what overperforms your average → tweak with sales elements → amplify with paid budget. This is the "brandformance" model. With post-iOS 14.5 tracking limitations, creative quality is even more important for paid — the algorithm does the targeting, your content must do the selling. Test organic first, amplify winners.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: you have organic content that overperformed your average, you have even a small ad budget ($100+/month), you want to drive direct sales/leads
- AVOID WHEN: you haven't been posting organically for at least several weeks, you're trying to amplify content that didn't resonate organically
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:OR]`, `[DTA:QQ]`, `[DTA:HK]`
**Key Examples:**
- Plumber with $700/month: Find 7 overperforming organic videos → tweak with sales CTA → spend $100 each within 5-mile radius → find 2 best → double down next month
- Facebook Advantage+ Shopping Campaigns: Feed up to 150 creative combinations, algorithm distributes to best audiences
- Arts nonprofit: Content that normally gets 50-100 views but 3 clips got 400-500 → spend $100 on those 3 as Instagram ads targeting 10-mile radius
- Lawn care business: 5 Facebook ads worked initially but leads declined → need 55 MORE ads with different angles, titles, hooks
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Establish your baseline: What's your average views/engagement across platforms?
2. Identify overperformers: Which posts got significantly more than average?
3. Tweak for sales: Add CTA ("email me for consultation," QR code, link in bio)
4. Start small: $50-100 per ad, targeted within relevant geographic/interest area
5. Compare performance: Which 2 of 7 ads drove the most results? Double down on those
6. Repurpose winning ads for other platforms: Add native elements (TikTok in-app title, etc.)
7. Ad fatigue is real: You need ongoing new creative, not the same 5 ads forever
8. Consider broad targeting + trust the algorithm (especially on Facebook/Instagram) — your creative IS the targeting
9. Don't dismiss platforms because they "don't convert" yet — TikTok ads are improving, like Facebook ads did in 2011-14
**Reversal:** Not all overperforming organic content makes good ads. Some content builds brand but isn't suited for direct response. Tweak appropriately. Also, for hyper-local businesses, you DO need geographic targeting — the algorithm can't always find your town on its own.

### [DTA:PA.1] Organic → Paid Pipeline
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 3
**Core Principle:** The best advertising strategy starts with organic content to discover what resonates, then amplifies winners with paid. This pipeline mitigates risk: you're spending money on content that's ALREADY proven to work. In a post-iOS 14.5 world, this is more important than ever because retargeting capabilities are limited. Building brand through organic content creates high-intent audiences that your paid campaigns can convert more efficiently at lower CAC.
**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: planning your overall marketing strategy, deciding how to allocate budget between content production and ad spend
- AVOID WHEN: you have a time-sensitive launch and need immediate paid reach (but still try to produce organic content alongside)
- COMBINES WITH: `[DTA:PA]`, `[DTA:OR]`, `[DTA:QQ]`
**Key Examples:**
- Facebook + Instagram: Post organic Reels → find overperformers → tweak with direct-response elements → run as paid ads with broad targeting, let algorithm optimize
- YouTube → CTV: Organic YouTube content → run winners as pre-roll ads → take to Hulu, Roku, connected TV
- LinkedIn B2B: 2-5 pieces of organic content daily → overperformers → run as LinkedIn ads targeting specific companies/job titles
- WineText.com: Using Facebook Reels to drive sign-ups for wine SMS service
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Produce organic content consistently (minimum several weeks)
2. Track performance metrics: views, engagement, watch time, shares
3. Identify content that significantly outperforms your average
4. Create paid versions: Add CTA, sales language, specific offers
5. Test small budget first → identify winners → scale budget on winners
6. Use organic insights to inform higher-production campaigns (modern commercials)
7. Continuously produce new organic content to feed the pipeline
**Reversal:** Sometimes you need to run paid ads even without organic validation — e.g., a product launch, a time-sensitive offer. In these cases, create multiple ad variations and let the algorithm find what works (this IS the validation, just faster and costlier).
