# Day Trading Attention — Content Creation & Monetization

## Content Strategy Fundamentals

### Strategic Organic Content (SOC) — The Framework
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 3 (SOC section)
**Tags:** `[DTA:QQ]`, `[DTA:HK]`, `[DTA:PN]`, `[DTA:CT]`

SOC is content that builds brand, grows community, and creates relevance. It's NOT "spray and pray." Every post should be:
1. Strategic — first 3 seconds, copy, format, platform features all optimized
2. Cohort-targeted — made for specific consumer segments, not "everyone"
3. Platform-native — uses in-app features, matches tone and context
4. Data-informed — builds on learnings from prior posts and comments

SOC serves THREE simultaneous purposes:
1. Builds brand and relevance from day one
2. Identifies content to amplify with paid (overperformers → ads)
3. Provides insights for higher-production content (what messaging resonates)

## Content Formats & Creative Styles

### [DTA:CT.2] Content Format Catalog
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 5
**Core Principle:** Different content formats appeal to different audiences and work differently on different platforms. Mastering multiple formats gives you more opportunities to resonate. Below are proven formats from the book with tactical notes.

**Straight-to-Camera Selfie Videos:**
- Simplest format. Pull out phone, talk about what you believe in, post it.
- Can combine with activities (walking, hiking, gym)
- "Posting is better than not posting" — don't wait for perfect conditions
- Break the fourth wall: share personal stories, milestones, genuine moments
- Builds closest connection with audience

**Green Screen Videos:**
- Layer your commentary over images, articles, screenshots, other videos
- Native feature on multiple platforms (TikTok, Instagram, Snapchat)
- Easy to scale: find relevant headline → screenshot → record reaction
- Use for: reacting to news, commenting on articles, reviewing products
- VaynerX strategy: Write articles specifically for influencers to green-screen for extra distribution

**Street Interviews:**
- Raw, spontaneous, authentic format
- Similar to Humans of New York (photo + story) but in video form
- Ask questions related to your business category
- Switch up question order for better hooks (salary → profession instead of profession → salary)
- Film at events, conferences, or literally on the street

**Skits:**
- Describe real-life scenarios people experience
- Call out the "elephant in the room" in your industry
- Don't need multiple people — play multiple characters yourself
- Tim Davidson on LinkedIn: B2B sales frustrations as skits
- Burberry x @sylvaniandrama: Luxury brand + casual tone = audience couldn't believe it was an ad

**Mascot-Driven Content:**
- Empire State Building: TikTok "eyes and mouth" filter on the building
- Duolingo: Mascot content dominates TikTok, drives actual business growth
- Start simple: in-app face filters on products → build personality over time
- Especially powerful for introverts who don't want to be on camera
- Can lead to merchandise division later
- GaryVee prediction: social media will reignite mascot usage like 1950s-70s advertising

**Carousels:**
- Instagram: Multiple chances for engagement (platform resurfaces tiles you haven't seen)
- TikTok: Swiping past last slide leads to profile
- LinkedIn: PDF document upload functions as carousel
- Tile 1 = hook (include "swipe for more" indicator)
- GaryVee finding: Meme/quote as tile 1 + video as tile 2 reaches more than video alone as Reel
- Decision trees across multiple tiles (Brit + Co example)
- Roundup posts ("10 moments that made us smile this week")

**Listicles:**
- "Top 5 icks about Ryanair" — numbering keeps people watching
- Combine with self-deprecating humor for humanizing effect
- Text-to-speech narration for easy consumption
- Works across all platforms in video or text format

**"Did You Know" Posts:**
- Creates curiosity — people want to discover what they don't know
- "I wish more DIYers knew..." → called out cohort + curiosity = millions of views on concrete company
- Address biggest misconceptions in your industry
- What mistakes do customers make trying to DIY?
- Strong POV + thorough explanation = high watch time even on 5+ minute videos

**Content with Multiple Actions:**
- Doing two things at once creates fascination (Hot Ones, Cold as Balls)
- Talking while doing makeup, cooking, working out
- Multiple camera angles in one video (split screen)
- Podcast ideas: "Brushing up on..." (literally brushing teeth while talking)
- Creates multiple hooks/reasons to watch

**User-Generated Content (UGC):**
- Incentivize customers to create content about your product
- Tie to challenges, giveaways, hashtag campaigns
- Russ example: Open verse challenge → duets → chose winner for official remix
- "Make a video with this hashtag for a chance to win..."
- Creates ambassadors for your business

**Reaction Videos:**
- Duets (side-by-side) and stitches (video after clip) on TikTok
- Similar features on YouTube, Facebook, Instagram
- Lawyer analyzing accidents, chef analyzing recipes, artist reacting to beats
- Leverage existing popular content to add your expertise

**Podcast Clips:**
- Record long-form podcast → chop into clips for all platforms
- Use Instagram's collaborative post feature with guests
- Guest-focused clips reach guest's audience → follow-back potential
- Steven Bartlett labeled audio "Stop scrolling & watch this" — testing small platform tricks

**Memes:**
- Language of the internet — underestimate at your own peril
- Work on LinkedIn too (Morning Brew example)
- Relate to common scenarios your cohorts experience
- Tie to current events for extra relevance (Druski x Rihanna Super Bowl)
- Can be image-only, GIF, or video format

**Notes App Posts:**
- Screenshot thoughts from phone's notes app → post as image
- Authentic, "straight from the heart" feel
- Easy for anyone to execute with zero design skills
- Repurpose same text across platforms in different formats

**Throwback Content:**
- Old photos with story in copy
- Origin stories, early days of business
- Humanizes professional brands
- "Throwback Thursday" / "Flashback Friday" for regularity

**Quick Takes / Opinions:**
- Share honest opinions about products/services in your industry
- Wine Library: GaryVee would criticize wines he SOLD in his own store
- @CarsJudge: Thumbs up/down on cars with sound effects — fast, visual, easy to understand
- Build trust through honesty, even when it doesn't benefit you directly

**Polls & Questions:**
- Best way to get content ideas: LITERALLY ASK THE AUDIENCE
- Platform polls (YouTube Community, Instagram Stories, LinkedIn)
- State strong POV → comments disagree → you learn what people actually think
- BlackRock example: 2,140 poll votes vs. just 80 likes and 7 comments — polls drive engagement from passive viewers
- "Pick a Life: A or B" looping video format for higher watch time

## The Jab, Jab, Jab, Right Hook Balance

### [DTA:CT.3] Value vs. Sales Balance
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 3
**Core Principle:** Updated from Jab, Jab, Jab, Right Hook (2013): Balance free value (jabs) with asks for business (right hooks). Too many asks = people tune you out. Only value and never asking = leaving money on the table. No set formula — every business finds its own balance. Take overperforming organic content and turn into sales-focused ads. Occasionally post directly sales-focused content organically.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Most content = provide value, build relevance, educate, entertain
2. Periodically = direct ask for business (buy, sign up, call, visit)
3. Run overperforming organic content as paid ads WITH sales elements added
4. For nonprofits specifically: ~50% awareness content, ~50% direct donation asks
5. Never be 100% in either direction
6. The modern twist: With interest-graph algorithms, a single piece of content can build brand AND drive sales simultaneously (like Doggface's Ocean Spray video)

## Show Different Sides

### [DTA:CT.4] Personal Content as Business Strategy
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 3 + Part 6
**Core Principle:** Showing different sides of yourself (hobbies, family life, passions outside work) is not unprofessional — it's a competitive advantage. One post about being a stay-at-home parent might get more views than all your professional content combined. People who see you on multiple topics across multiple platforms have a DEEPER relationship with you. The 80/20 rule: 80% your standard content, 20% something completely different — that 20% might become your next big thing.
**Key Examples:**
- GaryVee: wine → business → garage sales → NY Jets → blueberries → mindset — ALL works
- B2B hardware company executive posting about sneaker collecting → their customers' KIDS share it with parents
- GaryVee's blueberry video: "wouldn't have made sense to anyone, including me" — one of most successful posts ever
- Fashion YouTuber + music passion → don't choose, do BOTH
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Show your life beyond work — hobbies, passions, family (to your comfort level)
2. 80/20 rule: 80% core content, 20% experiments with different topics
3. Don't fear losing followers — with interest graph, content FINDS its audience
4. Different platforms = opportunity to show different sides
5. Authenticity beats "staying in your lane" — people relate to whole humans, not narrow experts
6. Self-deprecating content humanizes brands: talk about failures alongside successes

## Storytelling & Emotion

### [DTA:HK.2] Storytelling That Retains
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 3
**Core Principle:** "People will binge-watch Game of Thrones for hours. They'll also scroll past a 30-second YouTube Short." It's not about the LENGTH — it's about whether people want to watch. Content must be informative, thought-provoking, entertaining, or a combination. Once you catch attention with a hook, keep their attention through the video. What's their incentive to watch to the 30-second mark? The 1-minute mark? The 3-minute mark?
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Content length is NOT the enemy — engagement is
2. Ask: "Why would someone watch the next 10 seconds?"
3. Build in "information gaps" — tease what's coming
4. Use emotional storytelling: humor, inspiration, surprise, nostalgia
5. Short content that loops (auto-repeat on TikTok) can get higher watch-time metrics
6. Five-minute video CAN get millions of views if thoroughly valuable (Stellar Sidewalks concrete video)

## Copy Strategy

### [DTA:HK.3] Copy Optimization
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 3
**Core Principle:** Copy (the caption/text) is often overlooked but can be the PRIMARY driver of distribution. As platforms become search engines (TikTok, Instagram search), copy plays a huge role in discoverability. Copy can add context, call out cohorts, create curiosity, incentivize engagement, and serve as a "second hook." Platforms are expanding character limits — a sign they value thorough copy.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Copy can be the "CTA to watch" — "read the caption" in video drives watch time + reading
2. Start copy with a line for your cohort: "For all the dads out there"
3. Add curiosity/cliff-hanger that incentivizes clicking "see more" on LinkedIn
4. Reference platform-specific actions in copy
5. Use SEO-relevant keywords in copy for search discoverability
6. Text-only posts work on Facebook, LinkedIn, X, YouTube Community
7. Long, thorough copy on LinkedIn — users are in reading mode
8. Copy supports shareability: "The person who sent you this wants you to know..."

## Profile Optimization

### [DTA:PN.2] Profile Hygiene
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 3
**Core Principle:** When content performs well, thousands of people click through to your profile. If profile isn't optimized, you lose potential customers. People must easily see who you are, what you do, and how to take action (click link, call number, sign up). "Our job as operators is to eliminate friction."
**Key Examples:**
- ATL Mixers: 19.9M views on TikTok but link in bio → Instagram, no website, no phone number → massive revenue loss
- 81st Street Deli: Great pinned TikTok videos showcasing popular items, but missed opportunity on Instagram (no pinned posts, no Highlights)
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Bio: Clear description of what you do
2. Link: Website URL (or link-in-bio service)
3. Phone/email: If relevant to your business
4. Pinned content: Pin best-performing or most representative content at top
5. Highlights (Instagram): Showcase products, reviews, behind-the-scenes
6. Broadcast channel (Instagram): For direct updates
7. Profile picture: Clear, recognizable
8. Cross-platform links: Each profile should lead to your main website, not other social profiles

## Repurposing System

### [DTA:RP.1] The Content Production System
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Parts 3-6
**Core Principle:** The most efficient content system: Record long-form → chop into multiple platform-native pieces. One podcast interview = YouTube long-form + YouTube Shorts + Instagram Reels + TikToks + LinkedIn posts + X threads + Facebook Reels + newsletter content. For events: Film everything from every angle. "Sawdust" from production shoots = dozens of additional content pieces.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. **Long-form source**: Weekly/biweekly podcast or video series
2. **Capture everything**: Film events, meetings (when appropriate), behind-the-scenes
3. **Clip extraction**: Find 5-15 best moments from each long-form piece
4. **Platform adaptation**: Adapt each clip with native elements for each platform
5. **The sawdust**: During any production, capture additional content (interviews, BTS, reactions)
6. **Event filming**: Walk out with 40-70+ moments from a single event
7. **Rolling Stones Rule**: Repost winning content weeks/months later — it still resonates
8. **A/B versions**: Same video with different hooks, titles, or formats for different platforms

## Monetization & Business Results

### [DTA:PA.2] Brandformance Model
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 3
**Core Principle:** The false dichotomy of "brand" vs. "performance" marketing is dead. Modern advertising can do both simultaneously. Build brand through relevant organic content → this drives down CAC on your performance ads → take overperforming organic content → add direct-response elements → run as paid ads. The more aware people are of your product through organic content, the less your cost per acquisition in paid campaigns.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Brand building through organic content makes ALL paid advertising cheaper
2. People who've seen your organic content are more likely to click your paid ads
3. Take organic overperformers → add CTA (email, phone, QR code, "link in bio") → run as ads
4. Dollar Shave Club: $4,500 video = brand building + 12,000 orders in 48 hours
5. Purple Mattress: "Raw Egg Test" video = brand + $65.5M in sales first year
6. True Classic Tees: Comedy videos with 20% off CTA = brand + direct response

### [DTA:PA.3] Ad Fatigue & Creative Volume
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 3 (Amplification section)
**Core Principle:** The #1 mistake in paid advertising: Running the same few ads until they stop working, then blaming the platform. Ad fatigue is real — when people see your ad too many times, they stop paying attention. The solution is MORE CREATIVE, not more budget. A lawn care business with 5 declining ads needs 55 more, not a bigger budget. Small variables differentiate winning ads: the way you say something, the actor, one word in the title, the thumbnail.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Track when ad performance starts declining — this is ad fatigue
2. Solution: Create MORE creative variations, not increase budget on same ads
3. Different angles, different sayings, different titles, different hooks, different people
4. Narrow product references work: "Japanese maple" content might catch Japanese maple owners that nothing else would
5. Constant organic posting = constant pipeline of potential ad creative
6. One small variable change (word, actor, title, thumbnail) can turn a failing ad into a winner
7. Meta Advantage+ allows up to 150 creative combinations — feed the machine content

## Modern Commercials

### [DTA:PA.4] Videos People Want to Watch
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 3 (Modern Commercials section)
**Core Principle:** Modern commercials are higher-production videos that feel like scenes from a sitcom, music video, or movie trailer — NOT traditional vanilla car ads. They evoke emotion: laughter, sadness, happiness, joy. Dollar Shave Club ($4,500 video, 12K orders in 48 hours) and Purple Mattress ("Raw Egg Test," $65.5M first year sales) are paradigm examples. Concepts should be VALIDATED through organic content insights before investing in production.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Use organic content insights to identify what concepts resonate BEFORE production
2. Make multiple versions: one funny, one inspiring, one sentimental — post all, see which wins
3. Distribution: YouTube pre-roll + OTT (Hulu) + CTV (Roku) + vertical versions on social
4. Don't just make one big commercial — maximize the production shoot (capture "sawdust")
5. Add direct-response elements: QR code at end, "link in bio," website mention
6. Test QR code vs. website link versions — run both through YouTube pre-roll to see which drives more traffic
7. Can include influencers, "alpha moms," team members, or mascots
8. Self-deprecating humor: Ryanair calling out own flaws = humanizing, stands out from vanilla competitors

## Beyond Social Media: Extended Marketing

### Event Marketing
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 6
**Core Principle:** Events are a "twofer" — build relationships IN-PERSON while capturing footage for content. Set up activations at relevant events (Coachella, conferences, sports events). Film everything. Post clips across platforms. The activation itself should be designed with content capture in mind.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Set up activation with TWO priorities: (1) Make it relevant to attendees, (2) Film everything
2. Hire 2+ videographers for different angles
3. Ask interview questions that have viral potential
4. Q&A booths, tastings, challenges — interactive = better content
5. Even small events: film the local author reading, film the trivia night
6. Every event you attend or host = content opportunity

### Merchandise & Collaborations
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 6
**Core Principle:** Marketing through apparel and merchandise creates "walking billboards" that actually have cultural cachet. Collaborate with emerging apparel brands (not established ones) for credibility with younger demos. Blockbuster Video hoodie became popular AFTER the company died. Bass Pro Shops hat. Harley-Davidson merch. If a napkin brand collabed with an emerging streetwear brand, it gets "cool points."
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Partner with EMERGING brands (not Kith/Fear of God — too expensive, too mainstream)
2. Create limited-edition collaborative items: hoodie, hat, socks, T-shirt
3. People who buy become walking brand ambassadors
4. "Cool points" impact buying decisions subconsciously
5. Even commodity products (napkins, water) can benefit from merch strategy
6. Mascot merchandise as extension of social content strategy

### Collectible Products
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 6
**Core Principle:** Collecting is becoming pop culture — sneakers, trading cards, NFTs, vintage clothes. Brands can use collectibles to reach NEW audiences who buy the product for the collectible, use it, and become fans. Historical precedent: Cracker Jack toys, McDonald's Happy Meal toys, Capri Sun x Nickelodeon cards. Modern version: Gamer rookie cards inside product packaging.
**Actionable Framework:**
1. Include collectible items in product packaging
2. Partner with relevant cultural figures (Twitch gamers, emerging athletes)
3. "Rookie cards" of cultural figures = collector value + brand trial
4. Limited editions create urgency and word-of-mouth
5. Digital collectibles (NFTs) as additional format
6. Creates "trial" of product under cool cultural context (better than sampling at Costco)

## Team Building for Content

### [DTA:QQ.2] Building a Content Team
**Source:** Day Trading Attention, Part 6
**Core Principle:** Before hiring a team, you MUST understand the craft yourself. Spend months practicing so you can evaluate others. Don't just delegate to your "22-year-old niece" or a cheap agency without knowing how to judge their work. This isn't about age — some 20-year-olds are great, some 57-year-olds are great. It's about SKILL.

**The 4 key hires:**
1. Videographer (film + edit clips)
2. Copywriter
3. Designer
4. Strategic paid media person (understands SOC, PAC, and ad platforms)

**Hiring sequence:** Start with videographer (film podcast → edit clips → get content flowing). Add others as you get more sophisticated.

## Key Anti-Patterns (What NOT to Do)

1. **"Shadow ban" thinking** — 99.99% of the time, your content just isn't good enough. It's not a conspiracy. Make new, different, better stuff.
2. **Same content forever** — If reach is declining, CHANGE your formats, topics, hooks, styles. Don't keep doing the same thing expecting different results.
3. **Lazy cross-posting** — Copy-pasting identical content across platforms without adapting = leaving opportunity on the table.
4. **Promoting before providing value** — Every post is about your product = people tune you out. Build relevance FIRST.
5. **Waiting for perfection** — "Just start" is the message. A video recorded on a park bench with background noise can resonate if it's authentic and valuable.
6. **Not filming events** — Every in-person interaction is a content opportunity. Walk out of every event with 40-70 moments captured.
7. **Corporate/brand guidelines over relevance** — Making content that satisfies internal stakeholders but consumers ignore.
8. **Judging too early** — Don't say "it's not working" until you've posted consistently for 50-100 days.
9. **Overpaying for celebrity endorsements** — One A-list celeb post often drives less sales than a strategy with multiple micro-influencers.
10. **Not reading comments** — Comments are your free focus group. They tell you what to make next.
